## Read (fill in)
- dominant palette across the tiles you like: **near-mono on a true near-black ground with ONE warm metal hue** (tiles 01/06/08/10/16). Not "dark grey UI" — the ground goes to almost zero and the brass is the only chroma in the frame. Relationship, not eyedropper: one warm hue at high value against ~L0.12 ink, plus a single cold specular. Ivory paper (03/22/23) is the *second* world, used flat and bright, never blended into the dark one.
- light / contrast character: **one hard source, steep falloff, specular running along a curved edge.** Tiles 01/06/10 are lit by a single narrow bank; the object reads as a bright rim + a long dark body. Tiles 12/20 (Tillmans, Serpentine) are the same idea at architectural scale — one shaft of light in a black room. This is the `lighting:` for every 3D scene: hard key, no fill worth mentioning, background falls away.
- composition move worth stealing: **tile 22 — the mechanical-drafting line-type legend.** A page organised as ruled cells, each holding one dash pattern (visible / hidden / centre / phantom / cutting-plane) with a small-caps label. Steal the *rule vocabulary*, not the grid: the page's hairlines carry drafting meaning — solid = the object, dash-dot = an axis, long-dash-double-dot = a part that is elsewhere. Gives a whole graphic system that is earned by the subject instead of decorating it. → commit-sheet field 4.
- what to explicitly avoid from these: **the commercial "premium metal object on black seamless" still-life** (01, 06, 08, 09, 10, 16 are the same stock template six times — glowing gradient backdrop, object floating centred, soft studio sweep). That IS the reflex for this exact brief and the moodboard delivered it to my desk. Also avoid: the brass-instrument warmth becoming *orange*; the specimen sheets becoming a cream/beige page (ban #10). Junk to ignore: 05, 15, 18, 21.
